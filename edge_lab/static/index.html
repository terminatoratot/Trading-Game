The 30-second briefing

Odds b:1 mean a winning stake of 1 earns b profit plus your original stake back. A loss costs the stake.

EV per unit = p × b − (1 − p)

Positive EV when p > 1/(b+1). Estimate p, compare it with the price, choose a stake, then settle. Do not judge a decision from one outcome.

What to practise saying aloud

“The probability is approximately __. The break-even probability is __. My edge is __ per unit. I will stake __ because __. These bets share the same outcome, so my combined exposure is __.”

Know the rules.

Each board states its own rules at the top: read them, because some boards add a clue or use unusual dice. Unless a board says otherwise, dice are fair, six-sided and independent, and coins are fair. Cards come from a fresh, standard 52-card deck each round, without replacement. A=1, J=11, Q=12, K=13; face cards are J, Q, K.

  • Enter a probability for every proposition, as a percentage such as 41.67 or a fraction such as 5/12, and a stake in units. An estimate counts as correct when it is within ±10, ±5 or ±2 percentage points of the true probability on Easy, Medium or Hard.
  • Sum of stakes cannot exceed the bankroll. Zero or blank means pass.
  • Quoted odds are profit odds. Win: stake × odds in profit. Lose: minus your stake.
  • Interview countdown locks your entered bets automatically. Sprint's clock continues during review. Late submissions beyond a 2-second transport allowance are passed.
  • All propositions on a board use one shared outcome. Different rounds are fresh draws.
  • Coach: open hints one step at a time. Answers you give after opening the full walkthrough are left out of your pattern analysis.
  • Targeted practice and the targeted drill read your answer history in this browser, find your patterns (biased ranges, weak topics, overpriced buys, missed edges, bet sizing) and deal boards that test them.
  • Multiplayer: the host creates a room and shares its link or 4-letter code. Everyone plays the same boards and the same roll, and bets stay private until the round closes.
Single-bet Kelly = max(0, [p(b+1)−1]/b)

Kelly maximises expected log wealth for an isolated bet. The review's suggested stakes use half of the exact joint Kelly amount, which accounts for bets sharing one outcome.

Final P&L depends on luck. Track expected profit, money put into negative-EV bets, and probability-estimate error. Passing can be sensible, including when an edge is too uncertain.
